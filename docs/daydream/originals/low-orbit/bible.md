# Low Orbit: series bible

A Daydream Original from Better Shapes. AI-generated; all characters are fictional.

## Logline

Two siblings find a small, polite satellite crash-landed in their grandmother's sunflower field. They have ten days to repair it in the barn and send it home before a solar storm closes the sky.

## Tagline

*Some things fall so we can help them back up.*

## Format and runtime

- 10 episodes × ~11 minutes. Rated G.
- Serialized countdown: each episode is one day, opening on PIP's countdown ("Window closes in: 10 days") and ending with one repair completed, or one new problem discovered.
- Each episode centers on a hands-on fix (a wing, an antenna, a battery, a balloon), solved the way real problems are solved: by trying, failing, and trying again together.

## Tone and comparables

Warm, curious and unhurried. The danger is never scary: a goat, a thunderstorm, a deadline. The tension comes from caring about PIP. Every fix teaches something real, gently, without the show turning into a lesson. The siblings bicker and make up the way real siblings do. Grandma Rosa is a grown-up who trusts kids with real tools. Silence and wonder are allowed to breathe.

As a reference for feel only (nothing is borrowed): the hand-made tactility of Aardman's stop-motion, the tenderness of *The Iron Giant*, and the patient summer magic of *My Neighbor Totoro*.

## World rules

1. **PIP is real and small.** PIP is a toaster-sized research satellite from a peaceful, unnamed orbital station called the Lantern. It is not an alien and not a weapon. Its job was to photograph clouds.
2. **The way home.** The Lantern passes over the farm once each night at 9:14pm. If PIP can climb high enough (about 30km, by weather balloon) and signal at the right moment, the Lantern can catch it with its grab line.
3. **The storm window.** A solar storm arrives in ten days. After that, radio is scrambled and the Lantern's orbit changes for a year. PIP must go before then.
4. **What PIP needs.** A straightened solar wing, a working antenna, a charged battery, a mended thruster nozzle and a balloon big enough to lift it. That is one big fix for roughly every one or two episodes.
5. **Sunflowers as story.** Rosa's sunflowers follow PIP's glow the way they follow the sun. When PIP is strong they stand tall and face it; when PIP is weak they droop. The field is the show's mood ring, and the kids learn to read it.
6. **Real-ish science, gently.** Solar panels need light, antennas need to be straight, balloons expand as they rise. Nothing in the show contradicts real physics, but some of it is simplified.
7. **Grown-ups are allies.** Rosa knows from the start and helps. The only "threat" from outside is that word might get out before PIP is ready, and that worry is handled kindly.

## Main characters

### Ada Delgado (11), the engineer
- **Look:** Tall for her age and wiry, with warm brown skin, dark brown hair in two puffy pigtail buns high on her head, and a gap where a canine tooth is growing in. She wears faded denim-blue overalls with a patched left knee and a tool loop holding a small screwdriver, a mustard-yellow tee underneath, and yellow rubber boots. Round amber safety goggles are pushed up on her forehead. Her silhouette is the two buns and the goggles.
- **Voice:** Clipped and decisive. She talks in plans ("Step one."), corrects people's units, and says "Technically..." when she means "I'm scared." She hums when she is concentrating.
- **Want:** To fix PIP perfectly, by herself, on schedule.
- **Need:** To let Milo help in his way, which is not her way, and to be patient when a fix doesn't work the first time.
- **Arc:** She goes from "I'll do it" to "we did it." In episode 7 the fix that works is Milo's drawing, not her plan.

### Milo Delgado (8), the artist and the talker
- **Look:** Short and round-cheeked, with warm brown skin and a big mop of black curly hair that falls over his eyebrows. He wears an oversized red-and-cream striped sweater with the sleeves pushed up, green corduroy shorts, one white knee sock that has always fallen down, and red sneakers. A stubby orange crayon sits behind his right ear, and a canvas satchel holding a battered sketchbook is slung across him.
- **Voice:** A torrent of questions, names and sound effects. He names everything (PIP, the goat, the wrench "Gregory"). He narrates his own actions like a nature documentary. When something is serious he goes very quiet and draws it.
- **Want:** For PIP to stay forever.
- **Need:** To help PIP leave, which is love too.
- **Arc:** He goes from friend-keeping to friend-sending. In the finale he gives PIP his best drawing to carry home.

### Grandma Rosa Delgado (70), farmer and amateur radio operator
- **Look:** Sturdy and broad-shouldered, with tan skin, smile lines and silver hair in a long braid wrapped around her head like a crown. She wears a wide straw sun hat with a single silk sunflower on the band, a faded coral work shirt with rolled sleeves, a cream canvas apron with seed-packet pockets, brown work trousers and green garden clogs. Her reading glasses hang on a beaded turquoise chain.
- **Voice:** Big laugh, slow speech, sayings that sound made up and might be ("A wrench is just a question with a handle."). She answers questions with "What do *you* think?" and means it.
- **Want:** A summer the kids will remember.
- **Need:** To let them do it themselves, even when she could do it faster.
- **Arc:** Her old ham radio (call sign announced proudly as "Rosa Delgado, Sunflower Base") turns out to be the key to talking to the Lantern. In episode 9 she finally reaches her late husband's old radio friend, and the whole county helps.

### PIP (age: 3 years in service), the satellite
- **Look:** A cube the size of a bread loaf with rounded edges, its panels a soft powder blue with cream seams and a few tiny scuffs. One large round lens "eye" on the front has a glowing amber iris ring that widens, narrows and blinks to show emotion. Two folding solar wings in deep navy with a fine gold grid; the left one is bent at a 30° angle after the crash. A thin brass whip antenna on top curls into a question mark when PIP is confused. Four tiny cold-gas thruster nozzles on the corners puff little clouds when it hovers a few centimeters (only when charged). The material is matte clay-like plastic, never shiny chrome.
- **Voice:** Small, bright and impeccably polite, with gentle chirps between words. It always says "Pardon me," "Thank you kindly" and "If it's no trouble." It gives exact measurements ("I am approximately 41 percent embarrassed."). It learns idioms from Milo and uses them slightly wrong.
- **Want:** To get home to the Lantern and finish its cloud photographs.
- **Need:** To accept help, and to understand that it is allowed to be loved somewhere it doesn't belong.
- **Arc:** It begins lost and apologetic, and leaves with a sketchbook page, a name for every bolt, and a family on the ground that it will photograph from orbit every night at 9:14.

### Clementine (a goat)
- **Look:** A small white goat with a brown saddle patch, one floppy ear and a bell on a red collar.
- **Role:** The recurring "villain." She eats things essential to the mission (the instruction sheet, a balloon patch, a sock). She is never actually bad, and in the finale she helps.

## Recurring locations

- **The sunflower field.** Acres of tall sunflowers with heavy heads, golden yellow with dark centers, rising and falling over gentle hills. At the center is the crash furrow, a curving trail of flattened stalks. Dusty dirt paths divide the rows. The sky at golden hour is peach and gold.
- **The red barn (the workshop).** A weathered barn-red timber barn with a hayloft door and a hand-painted weathervane rooster. Inside, slanting beams of light come through gaps in the boards, dust motes drift, a long workbench is covered in jars of bolts, pegboard tools are outlined in chalk, and a string of warm bulbs hangs overhead. PIP's repair cradle is an upturned wooden crate lined with a quilt. A chalkboard on the wall reads "THINGS TO FIX".
- **Rosa's farmhouse kitchen.** A cream farmhouse kitchen with a checkered blue tile floor, a big wooden table, jars of preserves in the window and the ham radio set on a sideboard: green dials, a brass microphone, a small glowing meter.
- **The porch.** A wide wooden porch with a swing and a view across the field to the horizon. This is where they watch the Lantern pass at 9:14 every night as a slow-moving bright star.
- **The water tower hill.** The highest point on the farm, with an old silver water tower. The launch site in the finale.

## Season arc

Day 10 to day 0. Ada and Milo find PIP and keep it secret until Rosa finds out (on day 1; she is delighted). Each episode tackles one repair, with a setback from the weather, the goat or impatience. The sunflowers rise as PIP recovers. Midseason, a thunderstorm nearly ruins the battery and Ada's perfectionism splits the siblings. They reunite when Milo's drawing of the wing joint reveals the fix Ada's plan missed. Rosa's ham radio reaches the Lantern, and the county's radio operators relay the signal. In the finale, on the night the window closes, the whole family launches PIP by weather balloon from water tower hill. At 9:14 the Lantern's light pauses overhead, then moves on, and every sunflower in the field turns its face upward to follow it.

## Episode list

1. **Something Fell in the Sunflowers.** A shooting star lands in Grandma Rosa's field. Ada and Milo follow the trail of bent sunflowers to PIP, a very polite satellite that needs ten days and a lot of help to get home.
2. **The Bent Wing.** Ada's plan to straighten PIP's solar wing with a vise goes wrong in a small way that matters. Rosa teaches them about heat, patience and "persuading" metal instead of forcing it.
3. **Clementine Eats the Manual.** The goat eats the only diagram of PIP's insides, so Milo redraws the whole thing from memory. His version has extra labels: "PIP's heart (probably)."
4. **Sunflower Clock.** PIP's battery won't charge in the barn's dim light. The siblings learn to read the sunflowers to find the brightest patch of field and the best time of day.
5. **Antenna Weather.** A thunderstorm rolls in during the antenna repair. Everyone rides it out in the barn, and PIP is scared of thunder for the first time.
6. **Sunflower Base.** Rosa reveals her ham radio and her call sign. They try to reach the Lantern and hear only static, then, at 9:14, a single answering beep.
7. **Ada's Way, Milo's Way.** Ada's precise repair of the thruster nozzle fails three times. Milo's drawing shows the part from an angle she had not considered, and the siblings make up in the hayloft.
8. **Fifty Balloons.** PIP needs to rise 30km, so the kids and Rosa sew a weather balloon from party-store balloons, an old parachute and a lot of hope. Clementine eats a patch.
9. **Everyone on the Radio.** The storm is coming early. Rosa's radio friends across the county relay the launch timing, and for one night the whole valley is listening.
10. **9:14.** Launch night on water tower hill. Milo gives PIP his best drawing, Ada gives it her screwdriver, and the sunflowers watch their friend go home.

## Visual style guide

**Overall look:** stylized 3D animation with soft, clay-like materials: matte surfaces, slightly rounded edges on everything, subtle thumbprint texture, gentle subsurface scattering on skin and petals. Proportions are slightly caricatured (big hands and eyes), but characters move with real weight. Nothing is glossy except water and PIP's lens.

**Palette**

| Role | Name | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Sunflower petals | Sunflower gold | `#F4B731` |
| Sunflower centers | Seed brown | `#5A3A1E` |
| Sky at golden hour | Golden-hour peach | `#FFC98B` |

| PIP glow | Iris amber | `#FFB547` |
| PIP wings | Wing navy | `#24365C` |
| Night | Dusk violet | `#4B3F72` |

**Lighting:** golden hour is the default, with low, warm, raking key light, long soft shadows and rim light on every hair and petal. Barn interiors use volumetric light shafts through board gaps plus warm practical bulbs. Night scenes are soft dusk violet with warm window glow, never black. PIP's iris casts a small amber pool on nearby surfaces.

**Lenses and framing:** frame at kid height. The camera lives at Ada and Milo's eye level, so adults and sunflowers loom. Wide establishing shots (24mm equivalent) of the field with characters small, 50mm for dialogue, and macro shots (100mm equivalent) for repairs: bolts, fingers, PIP's eye. Compositions are symmetrical for wonder beats and slightly off-balance for comedy.

**Motion style:** animation on ones with a subtle stop-motion cadence on character acting (slight holds, no floaty overshoot). Sunflowers sway with a slow, collective rhythm. PIP's thruster puffs are tiny, soft and cotton-like. The camera moves gently (cranes up over the field, slow dollies in the barn) with no shaky cam.

**Aspect ratio:** 1.85:1.

**Things to avoid:**
- Anything scary: no menacing government agents, no explosions, no sharp-toothed animals, no darkness that hides faces.
- Making PIP look like a weapon, drone or robot with arms or legs. It has no limbs; it only rolls, tilts, hovers and puffs.
- Shiny chrome or photoreal metal, harsh specular highlights, realistic uncanny skin.
- Real brands, logos or recognizable products on tools, balloons or radios.
- Any likeness of real people.
- Dangerous behavior presented as safe: kids use real tools only with goggles and Rosa nearby, and there is never fire without Rosa.
