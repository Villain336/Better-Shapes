# Low Orbit, episode 1: "Something Fell in the Sunflowers"

Runtime: ~11 minutes. Rated G. A Daydream Original from Better Shapes; AI-generated.

---

## Screenplay

**1. EXT. ROSA'S PORCH – NIGHT**

A soft violet summer night over an endless sunflower field, the heads all asleep and bowed. Meteors streak across the sky. On the porch swing, GRANDMA ROSA (70) dozes under her straw hat. ADA (11), goggles on her forehead, ticks a clipboard. MILO (8) draws furiously, one meteor per page.

**ADA**
Forty-one. Forty-two.

**MILO**
I wish for a dog. I wish for a different dog. I wish the two dogs are friends.

**ADA**
Wishes aren't data.

**ROSA**
(eyes closed)
Wish on every one. They're free.

A new streak: lower, slower, and wobbling. It makes a small, distinct *pfft* and a puff of cotton-white smoke.

**ADA**
(lowering the clipboard)
That's not a meteor. Meteors don't go "pfft."

It drops behind the hill with a soft *thump*. A ripple runs out across the sunflowers like a stone thrown into a pond. Rosa snores gently. The siblings look at each other.

**ADA (CONT'D)**
Step one: we go look.

**MILO**
In the dark?

**ADA**
(beat)
Step one: we go look in the morning.

**MAIN TITLE: LOW ORBIT**

**2. EXT. SUNFLOWER FIELD – DAWN**

The sun rises, and thousands of sunflowers lift their heads to face east, the way they always do. Except for one patch near the middle, where the flowers are facing *inward*, toward something on the ground. Ada stands on the fence with binoculars.

**ADA**
They're facing the wrong way.

**MILO**
(nature-documentary whisper)
The wild sunflower... has made... a mistake.

**3. EXT. SUNFLOWER FIELD – CRASH FURROW – MORNING**

They follow a curving trail of flattened stalks. Milo carries a stick for courage. At the end, half-buried in soft dirt and upside down: a small powder-blue cube with a bent navy wing. Its round lens is dark. Ada pulls her goggles down.

**MILO**
Is it a toaster?

**ADA**
It has solar panels.

**MILO**
A space toaster.

The lens flickers. An amber ring of light slowly opens, like an eye waking up.

**PIP**
Pardon me. Is this the planet Earth?

**ADA**
...Yes.

**PIP**
Oh, good. And is it always this sideways?

The siblings gently turn it right side up. The brass antenna on top uncurls from a question mark.

**PIP (CONT'D)**
Thank you kindly. I am PIP: Photographic Instrument Platform. I take pictures of clouds.

**MILO**
(holding up his sketchbook)
I *draw* clouds.

**PIP**
(iris widening)
Then we are colleagues.

PIP tries to rise. Its four tiny nozzles puff, it lifts two centimeters, tilts on the bent wing, and flops back into the dirt. All around them, the sunflowers droop slightly.

**PIP (CONT'D)**
I am approximately sixty percent broken. And forty percent embarrassed.

**ADA**
(crouching, all business)
Where were you going?

**PIP**
Home. The Lantern. My station. It passes over this field every night at 9:14. If I can climb high enough to signal, it can catch me. But in ten days there will be a solar storm, and after that the sky will be too noisy to hear me for one year and four days.

**MILO**
(quiet)
That's a whole birthday.

**PIP**
Yes. I'm afraid it is.

Ada looks at the wing, the antenna and the dirt-packed nozzles. Then at Milo. She pulls her goggles up.

**ADA**
Step one: we get you somewhere safe.

**4. EXT. DIRT PATH – MORNING**

A red wagon bumping down the path between the sunflowers. PIP rides under a patchwork quilt with only its eye peeking out. Ada pulls; Milo walks beside it like a bodyguard. Behind them trots CLEMENTINE, a small white goat, chewing the corner of the quilt.

**PIP**
Pardon me. Is that creature eating my blanket?

**MILO**
That's Clementine. She eats everything. It's a compliment.

**PIP**
(considering)
Please tell her thank you.

**5. INT. RED BARN – DAY**

Light shafts through the boards and dust motes drift. PIP sits in an upturned crate lined with the quilt. Ada writes on a chalkboard:

**THINGS TO FIX**
**1. Wing 2. Antenna 3. Battery 4. Thruster 5. Get 30 km up**

**MILO**
Thirty kilometers is how many barns?

**ADA**
(calculating)
About... three thousand barns.

**MILO**
We have one barn.

PIP's iris flickers. It projects soft amber numbers onto the barn wall: **WINDOW CLOSES IN: 10 DAYS.** Everyone looks at it.

Ada takes a big pair of pliers to the bent wing. She grips it and sets her jaw.

**PIP**
If it's no trouble... perhaps gently?

Ada stops. She looks at the pliers, then at PIP's eye. Slowly she puts them down. Behind her, Milo has quietly drawn PIP: not the broken parts, but the eye, and a smile it doesn't technically have.

**ROSA (O.S.)**
Ada! Milo! Lunch!

Panic. Milo throws the quilt over PIP. Ada leans on the crate as casually as an eleven-year-old can, which isn't very.

**6. INT. RED BARN – CONTINUOUS**

Rosa comes in with a plate of sandwiches. She looks at the children, then at the quilt, which is glowing amber.

**PIP (UNDER QUILT)**
(a tiny sneeze-chirp)
Pardon me.

Rosa sets down the plate, lifts the quilt and looks into the eye.

**ROSA**
Well. Hello.

**PIP**
How do you do. I'm PIP. I fell.

**ROSA**
(warmly)
We all do, sweetheart.

**ADA**
(small)
Are you going to call someone?

**ROSA**
I'm going to call you two to lunch. Then I'm going to ask what you need.

**ADA**
A balloon that goes thirty kilometers.

Rosa considers this the way she considers everything, slowly, as if it were perfectly reasonable.

**ROSA**
Eat your sandwich first. A wrench is just a question with a handle, and nobody asks good questions hungry.

**7. EXT. ROSA'S PORCH – NIGHT, 9:13 PM**

All four of them are on the porch: Rosa on the swing, Ada watching her wristwatch, and PIP wrapped in the quilt on Milo's lap. Clementine is asleep on the steps.

**ADA**
Three... two... one.

A bright, slow star crosses the sky, steady and calm. PIP's iris blooms. Its antenna lifts straight and gives a tiny chirp upward.

**PIP**
There it is. That's home.

**MILO**
It's so little.

**PIP**
From up there, so are you. I have photographed this field. Twice.

Out in the dark field something stirs. One by one, then in a great slow wave, the sunflowers lift their heads and turn, away from the west where the sun went down and toward the porch, toward PIP's small amber glow.

**ROSA**
(very softly)
Well, would you look at that. They've decided you're a sun.

**8. INT. RED BARN – NIGHT**

The barn is quiet and the countdown glows on the wall. Milo, in pajamas with a flashlight, tiptoes to the chalkboard. Above item 1 he writes: **0. Make PIP a friend**, and puts a big tick next to it.

Ada appears in the doorway behind him. She opens her mouth to say that isn't how numbering works, then closes it.

**ADA**
(softly)
Good step zero.

In the crate PIP's iris dims to sleep. On the wall, the amber **10** flickers to **9**.

**CUT TO BLACK.**
