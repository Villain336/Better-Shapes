# Meadowlight: series bible

A Daydream Original from Better Shapes. AI-generated; all characters are fictional.

## Logline

In a small town where the sky answers any question asked before noon, a young meteorologist breaks the town's oldest agreement and asks where her vanished father went. The clouds answer: *above the weather*.

## Tagline

*Ask the sky. It always tells the truth.*

## Format and runtime

- 8 episodes × ~22 minutes. Rated PG for mild peril and themes of loss.
- Serialized, with a self-contained "question of the day" in each episode.
- Every episode ends at dusk, with the clouds answering. That is the show's signature beat.

## Tone and comparables

The show is warm, quiet and wondrous, like a painted landscape that has started to breathe. Nothing is in a hurry. The sky is enormous and the people are small and decent. Its grief is honest without being heavy, and its humor comes from neighbors who know each other too well. The magic is never explained by a lab coat; it is felt the way you feel weather coming.

As a reference for feel only (nothing is borrowed): the pastoral patience of Studio Ghibli's quieter films, the small-town tenderness of *Northern Exposure*, and the gentle strangeness of Ray Bradbury's Green Town stories.

## World rules

1. **The Asking.** Anyone standing on the Asking Stone on Weather Hill may ask the sky one question aloud, once per day, between sunrise and the noon bell.
2. **The Answer.** By dusk the clouds gather into words, a few at a time and in the order the questions were asked. The words are soft, rounded letters made of cumulus, lit apricot from below. Each answer holds for about a minute, then drifts apart.
3. **Always true, rarely long.** An answer is always true. These days it is never longer than five words, though the oldest volumes of the log record answers of whole sentences. The sky cannot lie, but it can be brief to the point of mischief.
4. **The present, not the future.** The sky answers what is true *now*. About the future it says NOT YET or ASK AGAIN. It will sometimes answer the question behind a question.
5. **The Keeper.** One person, the Keeper, logs every question and answer in the question log (31 volumes since 1891). By custom the Keeper keeps and does not ask.
6. **The Accord.** Since a hard autumn forty years ago, the town has agreed never to ask about "the ones who've gone." No one says that rule out loud; everyone knows it.
7. **The fading.** The log shows the answers getting shorter, decade after decade. Only a few people have noticed.
8. **The cloudless day.** Two hundred and eleven days before the pilot, the sky over Meadowlight was empty for the first time in living memory. That was the day Abel Calder vanished.

## Main characters

### Wren Calder (24), meteorologist and Keeper of the question log
- **Look:** Tall and narrow-shouldered, with a long stride. Copper-auburn hair in a loose low braid over her left shoulder, with flyaways at the temples. Light freckles and gray-green eyes. She wears a mustard-yellow oilskin raincoat (her signature, worn open) over an oatmeal cable-knit sweater, olive corduroy trousers and scuffed tan lace-up boots. On a leather cord around her neck hangs her father's brass pocket barometer, the size of a pocket watch. She keeps a pencil behind her left ear.
- **Voice:** Precise and dry, with weather vocabulary she uses without noticing ("that's a front coming in"). She asks questions in place of saying how she feels. When she is moved she speaks more quietly, never louder.
- **Want:** To find her father.
- **Need:** To stop living as the town's careful recorder and ask her own questions, about him and about herself.
- **Arc:** From Keeper to asker. She breaks the Accord in the pilot, follows her father's trail up into the sky in episode 7, and in the end chooses to come back down. She remakes the Keeper's role as someone who helps the town ask braver questions.

### Abel Calder (56), Wren's father and the previous Keeper (missing)
- **Look:** Broad and slightly stooped, with a close-cropped silver beard, a weathered face and deep smile lines. He wears a navy fisherman's rib-knit sweater with a frayed left cuff, a charcoal wool flat cap and a canvas satchel stuffed with notebooks. He appears in flashbacks and, late in the season, inside the clouds.
- **Voice:** Slow and warm, full of terrible weather puns he is proud of. He answers questions with better questions.
- **Want:** To keep the sky from fading.
- **Need:** To trust Wren with the truth instead of protecting her from it.
- **Arc:** He is revealed through the secret page and the flashbacks. He went "above the weather" to keep the sky's side of the conversation alive. In the finale he lets Wren choose her own way.

### Hattie Ames (71), postmistress and chair of the Town Council
- **Look:** Small and upright. White chin-length bob, round tortoiseshell glasses on a beaded chain, a plum wool cardigan over a high-necked cream blouse, a gray tweed skirt and black lace-up shoes. She always has a broom or a bundle of letters in hand.
- **Voice:** Clipped, formal and drily funny. She uses Wren's full name as a warning. She never raises her voice.
- **Want:** To keep Meadowlight gentle and unbroken.
- **Need:** To grieve her sister Clara, whose loss led to the Accord.
- **Arc:** At first she is Wren's opposition. In episode 4 her history comes out: forty years ago she asked about Clara and got an answer she could not bear. By the finale she stands on the Asking Stone herself.

### Silas Moreno (26), beekeeper and Wren's oldest friend
- **Look:** Medium build with sun-browned skin and dark curly hair cut short at the sides. He wears an untucked white linen work shirt with the sleeves rolled to the elbow, a waxed-canvas apron in faded sage, brown work trousers and a wide-brimmed beekeeper's hat with the veil pushed back. He carries a dented brass smoker.
- **Voice:** Easy and teasing, and he notices everything. He asks the sky nothing at all, on principle ("I'd rather be surprised").
- **Want:** For Wren to be all right.
- **Need:** To admit he wants more than that, and to risk being surprised.
- **Arc:** He is the skeptic and the anchor. He flies the balloon in episode 7, and the one question he finally asks in the finale is not about the sky.

### Tilly Brandt (10), the town's most frequent asker
- **Look:** Small, with a gap-toothed grin and white-blonde hair in two uneven pigtails tied with red yarn. She wears a cherry-red pinafore dress over a white-and-navy striped tee, green wellington boots, and a canvas satchel covered in hand-drawn cloud badges.
- **Voice:** Fast and fearless, and very literal. She has asked a question every day for 412 days in a row, mostly about her cat, Mister Buttons.
- **Want:** To be Wren's apprentice.
- **Need:** To learn that a question can be big.
- **Arc:** She starts as comic relief and becomes the heart of the town's courage. In episode 3 the sky answers her with a question of its own, the first time it has ever done that.

## Recurring locations

- **Weather Hill and the Asking Stone.** A round, grassy hill above the town, covered in blue poppies, lavender and pink and white cosmos. On top is a flat, lichen-spotted granite slab about two meters across, worn into a shallow dip where people stand. Beside it, under a canvas parasol, stand Wren's folding oak table and a brass noon bell hung on a weathered post.
- **Calder Weather Station.** A three-story round stone tower at the edge of town, with a copper weathervane shaped like a heron. Inside, a spiral wooden stair passes shelves of instruments (barographs, hygrometers, an anemometer on the roof). The 31 volumes of the question log stand on an oak shelf, bound in faded green cloth with gilt numbers. Abel's flat cap still hangs on its hook. The light is warm lamplight and the windows face west.
- **High Street.** A single gently curving street of honey-colored stone buildings with slate roofs and window boxes of geraniums: the post office with its green door, Okonjo's bakery, a hardware shop. Chalk drawings appear and disappear on the pavement.
- **Moreno Apiary.** A row of white and pale blue beehives at the edge of a lavender field, a low drystone wall and an old pear tree. Bees catch the light.
- **The Hedge.** The tall, ancient hawthorn hedge that marks the town boundary. Past it, the sky stops answering.
- **Above the Weather (episodes 7 and 8).** A sunlit landscape on top of the clouds: rolling cumulus fields like dunes, a soft blue-gold horizon and the quiet of high altitude. It is never dark there, and nothing in it is threatening.

## Season arc

Wren breaks the Accord and asks where her father is. The answer, ABOVE THE WEATHER, does not fade at dusk. It hangs over the town for days, and Meadowlight has to decide what kind of town it is. Wren and Silas follow Abel's secret questions. The sky is alive in a slow, weathery way. It is fading because the town stopped asking it anything that mattered. Abel went up to keep it company and keep it talking. Hattie's buried grief comes to the surface, and Tilly gets braver. In episode 7 Wren and Silas ride a patched hot-air balloon above the weather and find Abel, who is now partly cloud and content. Wren chooses to come home. In the finale the town gathers at the Asking Stone and asks the questions it had been afraid of. The sky answers them in full sentences for the first time in a century.

## Episode list

1. **The Question Log.** Wren finds a page proving her father secretly asked forbidden questions, and on the morning she finds it she asks the sky where he is. At dusk the clouds spell ABOVE THE WEATHER, and for the first time in the town's memory the answer stays after dark.
2. **Fair to Partly Cloudy.** The answer is still hanging over Meadowlight, so the Town Council meets in the post office to decide whether Wren has broken the town. She and Silas search the Weather Station for Abel's notebooks. In them they find a chart showing the answers getting shorter, decade after decade.
3. **Tilly's Weather.** Tilly asks whether the sky is lonely. The answer comes back as a question, ARE YOU?, and the whole town goes quiet. Wren makes Tilly her apprentice, and they learn that the sky is listening as well as answering.
4. **The Unasked.** Hattie tells Wren about her sister Clara and the question that created the Accord forty years ago. The two women read the old Keeper's log together. Hattie keeps a letter she has never sent.
5. **Lightning Season.** A summer storm rolls in and the answers turn to thunder. Wren and Silas fly an instrument kite into the storm's edge and bring back Abel's lost barometer, which is still ticking. It shows a pressure that cannot exist at ground level.
6. **The Cloudless Day.** The story of the day Abel vanished, told through Wren's memory and the town's. Each neighbor saw a piece of it. Put together, the pieces show that Abel chose to leave and that he left a goodbye nobody recognized.
7. **Above the Weather.** Silas patches his grandfather's hot-air balloon, and he and Wren rise through the cloud deck into the bright country above. Abel is there, gentle and mostly made of weather. He asks Wren the question he never could, and she answers it.
8. **Dusk Answers.** Wren comes home and the Accord ends. The whole town climbs Weather Hill to ask what it has been afraid to ask, Hattie and Silas included. At dusk the sky answers in full sentences, and the log gets a new first page.

## Visual style guide

**Overall look:** painterly realism. The images are photographic in light and depth, but softened as if painted in gouache: slightly simplified textures, gentle edge falloff, visible air in the distance. Skies take up at least half of exterior frames.

**Palette**

| Clouds | Cumulus cream | `#F4F1EA` |
| Cloud shadow | Soft slate | `#A7B0BE` |
| Dusk light | Apricot dusk | `#F2B48A` |
| Flowers | Blue poppy | `#5B8DD6` |

| Flowers | Cosmos pink | `#E39BB5` |
| Fields | Meadow green | `#7E9F5B` |
| Wren accent | Oilskin mustard | `#D9A23B` |
| Interiors | Lamplit umber | `#6B4E3A` |
| Night | Moonlit indigo | `#2E3558` |

**Lighting:** natural and motivated. Mornings are cool and clear with long shadows. Afternoons are soft and hazy. Dusk is the show's set piece: apricot light lighting the clouds from below and lavender shadows on the ground. Interiors are lit by warm lamps against cool window light. There are no hard, blown-out highlights.

**Lenses and framing:** wide primes (24–35mm) for landscapes, with people placed small in the lower third under a huge sky. Use 50mm for conversations and 85mm for close-ups, with shallow depth of field and flowers soft in the foreground. Keep horizons low, typically at 25–30% of frame height. Cloud answers are framed as wide, upward-tilted shots with the crowd silhouetted at the bottom edge.

**Motion style:** slow and patient. Use slow push-ins, gentle cranes up from the flowers to the sky, and handheld only for moments of emotion. Clouds move like real time-lapse at 4–8× speed, never faster. Wind moves through the grass in long waves. There are no whip pans or fast cuts, and a typical shot runs 8–30 seconds.

**Aspect ratio:** 2.39:1.

**Cloud lettering:** answers are built from real-looking cumulus. The letters are rounded capitals with no serifs and slightly lumpy, with sunlit tops and soft gray undersides. They form over about 20 seconds as the clouds pull together and dissolve the same way. Each word is legible but never looks like a typeface.

**Locations.** Reuse one locked establishing plate for each location: Weather Hill from the south-east, the Weather Station from the High Street, and the apiary from the lavender rows. The Asking Stone always has the brass noon bell to camera-left of it and the oak table to camera-right.

**Things to avoid:**
- Raised fonts, glowing neon or smoke-writing in the cloud answers. They must look like weather.
- Rain or storms outside episode 5. Dark, threatening skies anywhere.
- Contemporary branded clothing, phones held up to the sky, cars on the High Street. The town has bicycles and one delivery van, which is pale green.
- Changes to Wren's hair color, the color of her coat or the side her braid falls on.
- Any likeness of a real person. Prompts must never reference real actors or public figures.
- Oversaturated "fantasy" color grading. The wonder comes from scale and light, not saturation.
