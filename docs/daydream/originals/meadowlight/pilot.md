## Screenplay

**COLD OPEN**

**1. EXT. WEATHER HILL – THE ASKING STONE – MORNING**

A tremendous sky, still empty. Wildflowers: blue poppies, lavender and cosmos, all moving in one long wave. Under a canvas parasol, WREN CALDER (24) sits at a folding oak table with a heavy green ledger, *QUESTION LOG – VOL. 31*. A short line of townsfolk waits. On the Asking Stone stands TILLY BRANDT (10), hands cupped around her mouth.

**TILLY**
Is Mister Buttons thinking about me right now?

**WREN**
(writing)
"Buttons" with two T's?

**TILLY**
Like a normal person.

Wren writes: *No. 11,402. T. Brandt. Is Mister Buttons thinking about me right now.* Tilly hops down. MRS. OKONJO (60s, flour on her sleeves) steps up.

**MRS. OKONJO**
Did I leave the oven on?

**WREN**
Mrs. Okonjo, the bakery is four minutes away.

**MRS. OKONJO**
And I'm already up here, aren't I?

Wren writes it down. Down the hill, HATTIE AMES (71) unlocks the post office and glances up at the hill as if counting heads.

A brass bell on a post: NOON. Wren rings it twice.

**WREN**
Asking's closed. Thank you, Meadowlight.

**2. EXT. WEATHER HILL – DUSK**

The whole town is on blankets, like a picnic. Apricot light. Above them, cumulus slowly pulls itself together into soft, enormous letters:

**YES. OFTEN.**

Tilly leaps up as if she has scored a goal. The letters drift apart and new ones gather:

**YES.**

Mrs. Okonjo is already running downhill. Laughter. Wren copies each answer into the log, then glances up at one small cloud, round as a thumbprint, that drifts alone and spells nothing.

**WREN**
(under her breath)
You again.

**MAIN TITLE: MEADOWLIGHT**

**3. INT. CALDER WEATHER STATION – NIGHT**

A round stone tower. Brass instruments ticking. Thirty-one green volumes on an oak shelf. A charcoal flat cap on its hook, untouched. Wren eats toast over the open log. A knock. SILAS MORENO (26) leans in with a jar of honey.

**SILAS**
Thursday tax.

**WREN**
It's Wednesday.

**SILAS**
Then I'm early, and you owe me a biscuit.

He sets the jar down and studies her.

**SILAS (CONT'D)**
You're doing the thing.

**WREN**
What thing.

**SILAS**
Holding your pencil like it owes you money.

She looks at the shelf.

**WREN**
Two hundred and eleven days. Every one logged except one. The day the sky was empty.

**SILAS**
Wren.

**WREN**
I'm only saying. If anyone were allowed to ask about him...

**SILAS**
Nobody asks about the ones who've gone. That rule's older than both of us.

**WREN**
Rules are just questions people were scared of.

She stands, takes down VOL. 30, her father's last volume, and the spine gives with a soft crack. A folded page slips from inside the back board. Abel's handwriting is quick and slanting. She unfolds it, and Silas comes to read over her shoulder.

**INSERT – THE PAGE:** *Questions, kept separately. A.C.*
*Is the sky alive? LISTENING.*
*Is it fading? YES.*
*Why? NOBODY ASKS IT ANYTHING.*
*Can I help? COME UP.*
And on the last line, dated the cloudless day:
*Will Wren be all right?*
The answer space is blank.

Silence. The barometer around Wren's neck ticks once.

**SILAS**
He asked. He broke the Accord.

**WREN**
(very quietly)
It wasn't empty that day because something was wrong. It was empty because he asked about me, and it didn't answer.

**4. EXT. HIGH STREET – MORNING**

Honey-colored stone, geraniums, chalk drawings on the pavement. Wren strides past with the page buttoned inside her coat. Hattie is sweeping the post office step.

**HATTIE**
Wren Calder. You've your father's look this morning. I don't mean it kindly.

**WREN**
Did you know he asked?

The broom stops.

**HATTIE**
Everyone has a question they shouldn't. Most of us have the decency to keep it in our pockets.

**WREN**
The answers are shorter every decade, Hattie. It's in your own minutes. The sky's going quiet.

**HATTIE**
Then let it go quietly. Some of us have had enough of loud.

Wren holds her gaze, then walks on uphill. Hattie watches her go and leans the broom against the door. She takes off her apron.

**5. EXT. WEATHER HILL – THE ASKING STONE – LATE MORNING**

The line is longer today. Tilly is near the front. Wren doesn't sit at the table. She walks past the queue and steps up onto the Asking Stone. A murmur: *the Keeper doesn't ask.* Silas jogs up the path with his smoker still trailing.

Hattie arrives, out of breath, and stops at the edge of the stone.

**HATTIE**
Step down, Wren Calder.

**WREN**
I'm not asking about the gone. I'm asking about the missing. You wrote the Accord, Hattie. You know those aren't the same.

Hattie has no answer. Wren turns to the empty sky. Her voice is not loud.

**WREN (CONT'D)**
Where is my father?

The wind drops, and every flower stops moving. One breath later the noon bell rings by itself, a single clear note.

**TILLY**
(whispering)
Was that before the bell?

**WREN**
It was before the bell.

**6. EXT. MORENO APIARY – AFTERNOON**

White and pale blue hives at the edge of the lavender. Silas works a frame with bare hands, and the bees are calm. Wren sits on the drystone wall.

**WREN**
What if it says nothing? What if it's a cloudless day again?

**SILAS**
Then it says nothing, and you'll still have asked. You'll be able to sleep.

**WREN**
You've never asked it a single thing. Twenty-six years.

**SILAS**
I'd rather be surprised.

He glances at her and away.

**SILAS (CONT'D)**
Most days I am.

**7. INT. POST OFFICE – AFTERNOON**

Pigeonholes and slanting light. Hattie sits alone with a small framed photograph: a young woman laughing in a field of cosmos, CLARA, and on the back *1985*. Hattie lays it face down, then thinks better of it and sets it upright.

**8. EXT. HIGH STREET – AFTERNOON**

Tilly kneels on the pavement chalking giant letters, trying out answers: HOME? UP? ASK AGAIN? She erases them with her sleeve and tries again.

**9. EXT. WEATHER HILL – DUSK**

More people than have ever come. Hattie stands apart from the blankets. Silas sits beside Wren, and Tilly sits on Wren's other side, unasked. The light turns apricot.

The first answers form: **SOUND.** Mr. Pell, who asked about the bridge, exhales. Then: **YES. BRAVE TOO.** Tilly turns red.

**TILLY**
(to Wren)
I asked if you were scared.

The letters drift away. The sky stays empty for a long moment. Then, from every horizon at once, cloud begins to move inward, slowly and with great purpose. Three words, wider than the town:

**ABOVE THE WEATHER.**

Nobody speaks. Wren's hand goes to the barometer at her throat.

**HATTIE**
That's not an answer.

**WREN**
It's the truest thing it's ever said.

The apricot fades to lavender, then to indigo. Everyone waits for the letters to drift apart, as they have done every night for a hundred and thirty years. They don't. The moon rises, and the words hang there lit in silver.

**HATTIE**
(barely audible)
They've never stayed.

Wren opens the log on her knees and writes by moonlight: *No. 11,431. W. Calder. Where is my father. ABOVE THE WEATHER.* She pauses, then adds: *Still there at nightfall.*

The small round cloud from every evening drifts across the moon to the end of the sentence and settles there. Slowly it curls into a shape. It is a question mark.

Wren laughs, one astonished breath, then lifts her pencil and starts a new line.

**SMASH TO BLACK.**
