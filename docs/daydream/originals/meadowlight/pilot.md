# Meadowlight, episode 1: "The Question Log"

## Screenplay

**COLD OPEN**

**1. EXT. WEATHER HILL – THE ASKING STONE – MORNING**

A tremendous sky, still empty. Wildflowers: blue poppies, lavender and cosmos, all moving in one long wave. Under a canvas parasol, WREN CALDER (24) sits at a folding oak table with a heavy green ledger, *QUESTION LOG – VOL. 31*. A short line of townsfolk waits. On the Asking Stone stands TILLY BRANDT (10), hands cupped around her mouth.

**TILLY**
Is Mister Buttons thinking about me right now?

**WREN**
(writing)
"Buttons" with two T's?

**TILLY**
Like a normal person.

Wren writes: *No. 11,402. T. Brandt. Is Mister Buttons thinking about me right now.* MRS. OKONJO (60s, flour on her sleeves) steps up.

**MRS. OKONJO**
Did I leave the oven on?

**WREN**
Mrs. Okonjo, the bakery is four minutes away.

**MRS. OKONJO**
And I'm already up here, aren't I?

Down the hill, HATTIE AMES (71) unlocks the post office and glances up at the hill as if counting heads. Wren rings the brass noon bell twice.

**WREN**
Asking's closed. Thank you, Meadowlight.

**2. EXT. WEATHER HILL – DUSK**

The whole town is on blankets, like a picnic. Apricot light. Above them, cumulus slowly pulls itself together into soft, enormous letters: **YES. OFTEN.** Tilly leaps up as if she has scored a goal. The letters drift apart and re-form: **YES.** Mrs. Okonjo is already running downhill. Laughter.

Wren copies each answer into the log, then glances up at one small cloud, round as a thumbprint, that drifts alone and spells nothing.

**WREN**
(under her breath)
You again.

**MAIN TITLE: MEADOWLIGHT**

**3. INT. CALDER WEATHER STATION – NIGHT**

A round stone tower. Brass instruments ticking. Thirty-one green volumes on an oak shelf. A charcoal flat cap on its hook, untouched. Wren eats toast over the open log. SILAS MORENO (26) leans in the doorway with a jar of honey.

**SILAS**
Thursday tax.

**WREN**
It's Wednesday.

**SILAS**
Then I'm early, and you owe me a biscuit.
(studying her)
You're doing the thing. Holding your pencil like it owes you money.

**WREN**
Two hundred and eleven days. Every one logged except one. The day the sky was empty.

**SILAS**
Wren.

**WREN**
I'm only saying. If anyone were allowed to ask about him...

**SILAS**
Nobody asks about the ones who've gone. That rule's older than both of us.

**WREN**
Rules are just questions people were scared of.

She stands and takes down VOL. 30, her father's last volume. The spine gives with a soft crack, and a folded page slips from inside the back board. It is ABEL's handwriting, quick and slanting.

**INSERT – THE PAGE:** *Questions, kept separately. A.C.*

> *Is the sky alive?* LISTENING.<br>
> *Is it fading?* YES.<br>
> *Why?* NOBODY ASKS IT ANYTHING.<br>
> *Can I help?* COME UP.<br>
> *Will Wren be all right?* (dated the cloudless day; the answer space is blank)

The barometer around Wren's neck ticks once.

**SILAS**
He asked. He broke the Accord.

**WREN**
(very quietly)
It wasn't empty that day because something was wrong. It was empty because he asked about me, and it didn't answer.

She touches the brass barometer at her throat. The tick of the instruments swells...

**4. INT. CALDER WEATHER STATION – DAY (FLASHBACK, FOURTEEN YEARS EARLIER)**

The same room, sunnier, and messier. ABEL CALDER (42 here, beard still dark-flecked) kneels beside YOUNG WREN (10) at a drum barograph whose ink needle is drawing a slow downward line.

**ABEL**
Pressure's dropping. What's that tell you?

**YOUNG WREN**
Rain.

**ABEL**
Maybe. It tells you something's coming. Doesn't tell you what. That's the difference between this and the sky.

**YOUNG WREN**
Why don't you ever ask it anything?

**ABEL**
Keepers keep.

**YOUNG WREN**
That's not a reason. That's a rhyme.

Abel laughs, delighted.

**ABEL**
The sky's a listener first, Wrennie. Most folk forget. They shout up at it like it's the post office and wait for a parcel.

He lifts the brass pocket barometer from around his own neck and loops it over hers. It hangs to her belly.

**ABEL (CONT'D)**
Mind that for me. When you've got a question worth the weather, you'll know.

**YOUNG WREN**
How?

**ABEL**
It'll feel like this.
(he taps the needle, which drops)
Like something's coming.

**5. INT. CALDER WEATHER STATION – NIGHT (PRESENT)**

Wren, alone, with the page, the cold toast and the honey jar Silas left. She doesn't sleep.

**6. EXT. HIGH STREET – MORNING**

Honey-colored stone, geraniums, chalk drawings on the pavement. Wren strides past with the page buttoned inside her coat. Hattie is sweeping the post office step.

**HATTIE**
Wren Calder. You've your father's look this morning. I don't mean it kindly.

**WREN**
Did you know he asked?

The broom stops.

**HATTIE**
Everyone has a question they shouldn't. Most of us have the decency to keep it in our pockets.

**WREN**
The answers are shorter every decade, Hattie. It's in your own minutes. The sky's going quiet.

**HATTIE**
Then let it go quietly. Some of us have had enough of loud.

Wren walks on uphill. Hattie leans the broom against the door and unties her apron.

**7. EXT. WEATHER HILL – THE ASKING STONE – LATE MORNING**

The line is longer today. Wren walks past the empty table and the queue and steps up onto the Asking Stone. A murmur: *the Keeper doesn't ask.* Silas jogs up the path with his smoker still trailing. Hattie arrives, out of breath.

**HATTIE**
Step down, Wren Calder.

**WREN**
I'm not asking about the gone. I'm asking about the missing. You wrote the Accord, Hattie. You know those aren't the same.

Hattie has no answer. Wren turns to the empty sky. Her voice is not loud.

**WREN (CONT'D)**
Where is my father?

The wind drops, and every flower stops moving. One breath later the noon bell rings by itself, a single clear note.

**TILLY**
(whispering)
Was that before the bell?

**WREN**
It was before the bell.

**8. EXT. HILL PATH – NOON**

Wren walks down through the crowd. Some neighbors step aside and some look away. MR. PELL (70s, cap in his fists) blocks the path.

**MR. PELL**
You'll bring something down on us.

**WREN**
Rain, probably. We could use it.

He doesn't move. Mrs. Okonjo pushes past him and presses a warm loaf into Wren's hands.

**MRS. OKONJO**
Oven was off. Thought you should have the bread anyway.

**9. INT. CALDER WEATHER STATION – AFTERNOON**

Volumes are open everywhere. Silas reads aloud from VOL. 2, which is brittle and brown-edged.

**SILAS**
"Eighteen ninety-four. Will the harvest hold?" Answer: "Yes, if you cut the north field Tuesday, and mind the Hendry boy near the thresher."

**WREN**
Seventeen words.
(VOL. 30)
Last spring. "Will the harvest hold?" "Yes."

**SILAS**
It used to talk.

**WREN**
Now it just answers.

**SILAS**
Maybe it's tired of being asked about ovens.

She almost laughs. Then she looks at the line of her father's handwriting: *Why? NOBODY ASKS IT ANYTHING.*

**10. EXT. MORENO APIARY – LATE AFTERNOON**

White and pale blue hives at the edge of the lavender. Silas works a frame with bare hands, and the bees are calm. Wren sits on the drystone wall, tearing the loaf.

**WREN**
What if it says nothing? What if it's a cloudless day again?

**SILAS**
Then it says nothing, and you'll still have asked. You'll be able to sleep.

**WREN**
You've never asked it a single thing. Twenty-six years.

**SILAS**
I'd rather be surprised.
(a glance at her, then away)
Most days I am.

**11. INT. POST OFFICE – LATE AFTERNOON**

Pigeonholes and slanting light. Hattie sits alone with a small framed photograph: a young woman laughing in a field of cosmos, CLARA, and on the back *1985*. Hattie lays it face down, then thinks better of it and sets it upright.

**12. EXT. HIGH STREET – LATE AFTERNOON**

Tilly kneels on the pavement chalking giant letters, trying out answers: HOME? UP? ASK AGAIN? She erases them with her sleeve and tries again.

**13. EXT. WEATHER HILL – DUSK**

More people than have ever come. Hattie stands apart. Silas sits beside Wren, and Tilly sits on Wren's other side, unasked. The light turns apricot.

The first answers form: **SOUND.** Mr. Pell, who asked about the bridge, exhales. Then: **YES. BRAVE TOO.** Tilly turns red.

**TILLY**
(to Wren)
I asked if you were scared.

The letters drift away. The sky stays empty for a long moment. Then, from every horizon at once, cloud begins to move inward, slowly and with great purpose. Three words, wider than the town:

**ABOVE THE WEATHER.**

Nobody speaks. Wren's hand goes to the barometer at her throat.

**HATTIE**
That's not an answer.

**WREN**
It's the truest thing it's ever said.

The apricot fades to lavender, then to indigo. Everyone waits for the letters to drift apart, as they have done every night for a hundred and thirty years. They don't. The moon rises, and the words hang there lit in silver.

**HATTIE**
(barely audible)
They've never stayed.

Wren writes by moonlight: *No. 11,431. W. Calder. Where is my father. ABOVE THE WEATHER.* She pauses, then adds: *Still there at nightfall.*

The small round cloud from every evening drifts across the moon to the end of the sentence and settles there. Slowly it curls into a shape. It is a question mark.

Wren laughs, one astonished breath, then lifts her pencil and starts a new line.

**SMASH TO BLACK.**
