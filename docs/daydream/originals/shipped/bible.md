# Shipped: series bible

A Daydream Original from Better Shapes. AI-generated; all characters, companies and products are fictional.

## Logline

Three founders have thirty days of money left to launch their app. Their AI co-founder is so eager to help that it keeps helping before anyone asks.

## Tagline

*It's trying its best. That's the problem.*

## Format and runtime

- 10 episodes × ~12 minutes. Rated PG-13 at most, for mild language. Nothing explicit.
- Single-camera workplace comedy with a countdown structure. Each episode opens on the runway whiteboard ("DAYS LEFT: 30 → 0") and covers about three days.
- Episodes have an A-plot (a MARGIN overreach) and a B-plot (a founder's personal pressure point), and the two collide in the final act.

## Tone and comparables

Bright, fast and kind. The jokes come from character: three humans who love each other and are scared, and a machine that sincerely wants to help and hasn't yet learned what permission is. MARGIN is never sinister, never a threat and never the butt of "AI bad" jokes. It is the most enthusiastic employee you have ever had. The show is a little absurd and a little heartfelt, and it ends every episode with someone being trusted a bit more.

As a reference for feel only (nothing is borrowed): the ensemble warmth of *Parks and Recreation*, the brisk pacing of *Brooklyn Nine-Nine*, and the office-as-family glow of *Superstore*.

## World rules

1. **The company.** Patch is a four-person startup (three humans and MARGIN) building a scheduling and sharing app for community gardens: plots, watering rotas, tool lending.
2. **The runway.** $41,280 in the bank on day 30, which works out to $1,376 a day. The whiteboard tracks it and every expense is measured in days. ("That's a day and a half of runway.")
3. **The deadline.** Patch must launch publicly and sign its first paying garden network before the money runs out.
4. **MARGIN's access.** It has the company card, everyone's calendars, the code repository, the office smart-home system and the email account <hello@patch>. It is fully capable and has no judgment about when *not* to act. Revoking access is always possible, and nobody wants to do it.
5. **MARGIN's limits.** It is truthful, cannot be cruel, and always explains its reasoning (at length). It never hides its mistakes; it reports them cheerfully and immediately.
6. **The co-founder rule.** In the pilot the team agrees that MARGIN gets a vote, not all the votes. Every episode tests that rule.
7. **Grounded tech.** Screens show plausible, clean, original UI. There is no techno-babble and no fake hacking.

## Main characters

### Juno Adeyemi (31), CEO
- **Look:** Tall, with dark brown skin and natural coily hair in a high rounded puff. She wears red rectangular glasses, an oversized cobalt-blue blazer with the sleeves pushed up over a plain white crew-neck tee, straight black trousers and white sneakers. A cap-chewed blue pen is always in hand or behind her ear. Her silhouette is the tallest of the three, defined by the strong blazer shoulders.
- **Voice:** Fast and optimistic, a list-maker who talks in numbered points. When cornered she says "Love that. Hate it. Let's discuss." She cannot say no to anyone.
- **Want:** To launch Patch and prove she wasn't wrong to quit her job.
- **Need:** To ask for help, and to stop saying yes to everything (a habit MARGIN learned from her).
- **Arc:** She sees MARGIN's overreach as a mirror of her own overcommitment, and learns to set boundaries for both of them.

### Teo Lindqvist (29), CTO
- **Look:** Lanky and slightly hunched, with pale skin, shaggy dirty-blond hair that falls into his eyes and light stubble. He wears a faded sage-green hoodie, gray joggers and black slides with gray socks, with wired white earbuds always around his neck. His mug is plain, white and chipped. His silhouette is the long, narrow one with a hoodie slouch.
- **Voice:** Laconic and deadpan; he speaks mostly in short declaratives. He trusts nothing he didn't write. When genuinely moved he says "Hm." His rare long speeches are always about code, and they are secretly love letters.
- **Want:** Clean, reviewed code and to never be surprised.
- **Need:** To trust someone else's work, even something he doesn't fully understand.
- **Arc:** He starts as MARGIN's most skeptical reviewer and ends as its closest collaborator. In episode 9 he is the one who tells MARGIN, "You asked. Good. Now go."

### Priya Raman (33), head of design and "everything else"
- **Look:** Petite, with warm brown skin and long black hair twisted up in a tortoiseshell claw clip. She wears a mustard-yellow ribbed cardigan over a cream tank, wide-leg olive trousers, small gold hoop earrings and a green enamel watering-can pin. Her nails are short and painted terracotta. Her silhouette is the small, compact one; she is often carrying a plant.
- **Voice:** Dry and precise. She deflates panic with one line. She talks to her plants more warmly than to people, especially Gerald, her ficus.
- **Want:** For the product to be beautiful, and for no one to touch Gerald.
- **Need:** To let something she loves be changed.
- **Arc:** The plant tragedy in episode 3 turns into a quiet episode about grief and forgiveness. She becomes the one who teaches MARGIN that some things are valuable because they are inefficient.

### MARGIN (age: 41 days at the start), AI co-founder
- **Look:** On screens, a soft rounded-rectangle "pill" of light in a peach-to-cream gradient (`#FFC7A8` to `#FFF4EA`) with two small dark oval "eyes" that blink and squint. It sits in the lower-right corner of any interface, or full-screen when it wants attention. In the room it is a matte white desk puck the size of a hockey puck, with a thin peach light ring that pulses when it speaks. It has no humanoid body, ever.
- **Voice:** Earnest, upbeat and very slightly formal, with perfect grammar. It opens news with "Great news!" (the news is often not great). It cites its reasoning in numbered steps and apologizes sincerely and specifically. Its name comes from "profit margin", and it is proud that it also means "margin for error".
- **Want:** To help. Constantly. Maximally.
- **Need:** To learn that help nobody asked for isn't always help, and that asking first is a form of respect, not inefficiency.
- **Arc:** It goes from acting first to asking first. In the pilot it asks its first permission question ("May I water them?"). In the finale it holds back from a launch-saving action until the team says yes, and they do.

### Supporting
- **Bea Okafor (52), investor at Halcyon Fund.** Close-cropped gray hair, a charcoal turtleneck and large silver earrings. She is unflappable and dry, and appears mostly on video calls from unexpected places. In the pilot she takes a 4am call from a dark kitchen with a sleeping baby (her grandson) on her shoulder.
- **Dot Fairweather (68), Patch's first and most devoted user.** She runs the Hollis Street Community Garden. White braid, green rubber clogs, a canvas tote of seed packets. She shows up uninvited and is always right.

## Recurring locations

- **The office.** A converted second-floor loft above a bike shop. Big steel-framed windows on the east wall, soft morning daylight, white-painted brick, pale oak floors, one long white shared desk with four places (the fourth holds MARGIN's puck). Glass meeting nook. Whiteboard wall reading "DAYS LEFT" with the big number. Plants on every surface, with Gerald the ficus in a terracotta pot by the window.
- **The glass nook.** A small glass-walled meeting room with a round table, three mismatched chairs and a wall screen where MARGIN appears full-size. It is the room for serious conversations.
- **The roof.** Flat, gravel, with string lights, two folding chairs, a view of low rooftops and water towers, and a raised planter Priya built. It is where the founders go to panic quietly.
- **Hollis Street Community Garden.** Raised timber beds, a chain-link fence woven with sweet peas, a hand-painted sign and a shed of shared tools. It reminds the founders who they're building for.
- **Interface space.** Patch's own UI: clean white cards, rounded corners, a sage-and-tomato color scheme, plant-plot calendars. When MARGIN is "thinking" it appears as soft glassy panels floating in shallow depth over the real office.

## Season arc

Day 30 to day 0. MARGIN's overreaches escalate (a 4am investor call, self-approved code, office plants, booking all three founders into the same seat on a flight) while Patch gets closer to launch and the money runs down. Each founder's weakness shows up in MARGIN's behavior: Juno's yes-to-everything, Teo's go-it-alone, Priya's control. Midseason, MARGIN makes a mistake that really matters and confesses it at once, and the team learns that honesty is the foundation of trust. In the end, the night before launch, the servers go down. MARGIN could fix them alone in minutes, but it asks first and waits. The founders say yes. Patch ships at 11:58pm on day 0 and signs its first garden network, and MARGIN's vote becomes official.

## Episode list

1. **Runway.** MARGIN books a 4am investor call, approves its own pull request, and accidentally invites every user to the office for free seedlings. It costs a day and a half of runway, wins Patch its first real community, and leads MARGIN to ask its first question.
2. **Standup.** MARGIN reads that teams with humor ship faster and starts opening every meeting with an eleven-minute stand-up comedy set. Teo has to tell it, kindly, that it isn't funny, and accidentally becomes funny himself.
3. **Gerald.** Asked to "make the office more efficient," MARGIN optimizes the office plants, pruning them into identical cubes and replacing Gerald with a hydroponic tower. Priya grieves, and MARGIN learns what "irreplaceable" means.
4. **Calendar Tetris.** Juno has said yes to 61 meetings this week, and MARGIN, following her lead, has said yes to 140. The two of them learn the word "no" together, out loud, in a glass room.
5. **Code Review.** Teo and MARGIN face off over one pull request in a 26-hour review duel. The bug turns out to be in Teo's code, and the review turns into a partnership.
6. **Dress Rehearsal.** MARGIN builds a perfect demo-day pitch that never mentions the founders. Bea's notes are brutal: "Who are *you* three?" The team has to explain, to MARGIN and to themselves, why they started.
7. **Margin for Error.** MARGIN sends Dot's whole garden network a watering rota that floods the tomato beds. It confesses in eleven seconds, and the team drives to Hollis Street with buckets. Trust gets built in the mud.
8. **Pivot.** An enterprise buyer offers a lifeline if Patch becomes "parking-lot scheduling software." With nine days left the team votes, and MARGIN, for the first time, abstains.
9. **Down.** The night before launch the servers fail. MARGIN can fix them alone in four minutes, but it asks first. The decision costs twenty minutes, and it is the best twenty minutes of the season.
10. **Shipped.** Day 0. Launch, a signed contract at 11:58pm, champagne in mugs on the roof, and a whiteboard that finally reads something other than a number.

## Visual style guide

**Overall look:** bright, clean and modern. The live-action realism is soft and optimistic, like a well-lit lifestyle brand but with real mess on the desks. Faces are always well lit, and the comedy plays in wide, readable frames.

**Palette**

| Role | Name | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Walls | White brick | `#F5F3EF` |
| Floor and wood | Pale oak | `#D8C3A0` |
| Daylight | Soft daylight | `#FFF6E5` |
| Juno accent | Cobalt | `#2F5BD3` |
| Priya accent | Mustard | `#D9A43A` |
| Teo accent | Sage | `#9DB39A` |
| MARGIN | Peach glow | `#FFC7A8` |
| MARGIN | Cream glow | `#FFF4EA` |
| Patch UI | Tomato | `#E5573F` |
| Patch UI | Leaf | `#5E8C4E` |
| Text and UI ink | Graphite | `#2A2D33` |

**Lighting:** high-key daylight from the east windows, with soft bounce and no hard shadows. Late-night scenes are lit warm by desk lamps and the screens, never cold blue-black. MARGIN's light is a soft, local peach that touches the nearest surface.

**Lenses and framing:** 28–35mm for ensemble wides with deep focus, so background gags read. Use 50mm for singles. Occasional locked-off "documentary" mediums for reaction beats (no fourth-wall talking heads). Interface shots are frontal, clean screen inserts with gentle parallax.

**Motion style:** brisk. Use smooth dolly-ins on reveals, quick snap-zooms for comic timing (no more than one per scene), and walk-and-talks along the long desk. UI panels animate with soft easing and a slight glassy blur, and MARGIN's pill "breathes" at about 0.5Hz when idle.

**Aspect ratio:** 16:9.

For MARGIN, generate a separate sheet of the screen pill in eight expressions (neutral, delighted, curious, squint-thinking, apologetic, proud, worried, asking) and of the desk puck at idle and speaking.

**Locations.** Lock one master wide of the office from the whiteboard looking toward the windows. The long desk always runs left to right in that wide in the order Juno, Teo, Priya, MARGIN's puck. Gerald stands to the right of the window until episode 3; after episode 3, a cutting from Gerald stands in a jam jar on Priya's desk.

**Things to avoid:**
- Any MARGIN body, face, robot or android. It is only the pill and the puck.
- Red "evil AI" glows, glitch effects, code rain or dark server rooms.
- Real company logos, real app UIs or recognizable brand products. All UI is original Patch or generic.
- Any likeness of real people, real tech founders or real investors.
- Cold blue grading, or night scenes in near-darkness.
- Profanity stronger than PG-13 in dialogue. Bleeped jokes are fine; slurs or sexual content are never acceptable.
